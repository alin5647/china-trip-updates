🇲🇴 Macau — Oct 28 arrival, Oct 29 morning departure

Macau blends Cantonese Chinese + Portuguese heritage. Locals speak Cantonese, many also Portuguese, and good English in tourist zones.

Macau: Cultural Customs & Local Etiquette

  • Temples (A-Ma Temple etc.): Dress modestly (shoulders/knees covered). Do not point at statues with your finger; use an open palm. Do not take photos inside shrines if signs forbid it.
  • UNESCO historic centre (Ruins of St Paul's): Very crowded, move slowly, avoid blocking walkways for long photo sessions.
  • Gambling casinos: Photography strictly banned inside casino floors, even phone cameras. Dress code: no overly ripped clothing. You must show passport to enter casinos.
🚁 Drone critical warning — Macau: You CANNOT fly your DJI Flip over any of the 22 World Heritage sites including Ruins of St Paul's. Also no flying near airport, government buildings. Very limited safe open areas for aerial shots in Macau.

Macau: Useful Apps / Services

  • Experience Macao (official tourism app) — offline walking maps, bus routes, visitor crowd heatmap, restaurant guide, English interface.
  • Google Maps for walking navigation.
  • Taxi / local bus: Buses are cheap, cash or local card. Taxis are easy from Four Seasons (Cotai).
  • Payments: Credit cards widely accepted at resorts, but small Taipa village street vendors prefer cash (MOP, HKD also commonly accepted 1:1 rough).

Macau Top 10 Must-See / Must-Eat

  1. Ruins of St Paul's + Mount Fortress (大三巴牌坊, Dà Sān Bā Pái Fāng) — your golden hour shoot, the iconic baroque stone facade, climb the fortress ramparts for elevated photos.
  2. Taipa Village (Rua do Cunha) (氹仔旧城区, Dàng Zǎi) — pastel colonial houses, cobblestone lanes, this is where you eat most of the local snacks.
  3. Portuguese Egg Tart (Pastel de Nata) — the signature Macau treat, burnt caramelised top, flaky layered crust.
  4. Pork Chop Bun (猪扒包, Zhū Pá Bāo) — thick marinated pork chop inside crispy baguette, Tai Lei Loi Kei (your breakfast pick for Oct 29).
  5. Macanese African Chicken (非洲鸡, Fēi Zhōu Jī) — spicy coconut peanut sauce, the signature Macanese fusion dish (dinner plan, Oct 28).
  6. Minchi (免治, Miǎn Zhì) — classic Macanese ground beef hash, comfort food unique to Macau.
  7. A-Ma Temple (妈阁庙, Mā Gé Miào) — oldest temple in Macau, sea goddess temple, important cultural landmark.
  8. Senado Square (Largo do Senado) (议事亭前地, Yì Shì Tíng Qián Dì) — wave-patterned Portuguese mosaic pavement, colourful colonial buildings.
  9. Crab Congee (蟹粥, Xiè Zhōu) — creamy congee cooked with fresh crab roe and meat, great comfort snack.
  10. Kun Iam Temple (观音堂, Guān Yīn Táng) — quiet temple gardens, historical significance, less crowded than A-Ma Temple.

🇭🇰 Hong Kong — Oct 29 – Nov 1, Conrad Hong Kong

Hong Kong is your main photography base. Locals speak Cantonese; many people in Central / Tsim Sha Tsui speak good English.

Hong Kong: Cultural Customs & Etiquette

  • MTR / public transit: Queuing is taken very seriously. Stand on the right side of escalators to let people walk past on left. Do not eat or drink inside MTR stations / trains.
  • Dim sum teahouse culture (Lin Heung Tea House / Maxim's Palace): It's normal to share tables with strangers at old-school yum cha spots. Be patient, pushcart service at Lin Heung is traditional, just signal to the aunties.
  • Street photography: Generally very tolerant, but avoid close-up shots of people without consent, especially fortune tellers in Temple Street.
🚁 Drone rules — Hong Kong: Victoria Harbour, airport, Disneyland are restricted zones. For your trip: Dragon's Back / Shek O area is your best candidate to safely test your DJI Flip, still double check HK CAD drone map before each flight, never fly over crowds of people.

Hong Kong: Useful Apps & Services (your core toolkit)

  • MTR Mobile — official subway app, real-time train times, station exit maps, super useful for navigating Central / Mong Kok.
  • Citymapper — better than Google Maps for combined MTR + bus + ferry + tram routes.
  • Octopus / Tourist Octopus Card: #1 essential. Tap-and-go for MTR, buses, Star Ferry, convenience stores. Grab a physical tourist Octopus card at airport for reliability.
  • OpenRice — HK's local restaurant review app, great to check opening hours for noodle shops, roast goose.
  • MyObservatory — official HK weather, rain / fog alerts, very helpful for sunrise / sunset photo planning.
  • DiDi HK for ride hailing when tired after hikes.
