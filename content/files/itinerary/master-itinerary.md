## Before you go

### Passports and visas - read this first

Mainland China, Macao and Hong Kong have separate entry rules. As of the guidance checked for this document, US passport holders need a visa arranged in advance for mainland China (the tourist L visa; recent guidance describes 10-year multiple-entry visas as common for US citizens, with passport validity of at least six months and a blank page required). Hong Kong is visa-free for US passport holders for short visits, and Macao also admits US passport holders for short stays. Rules change - confirm your specific passports and any existing visas with the Chinese embassy/consulate or an official source before Oct 18, and carry the passport you booked tickets with, since major attractions use real-name, passport-checked entry.

### Money and payments

- Set up Alipay and WeChat Pay before you leave the US and link a card that does not charge foreign transaction fees. Small vendors, taxis and ticket mini-programs assume mobile pay.

- Carry some RMB cash in small notes for Huangshan shuttle buses, temple donations and rural stops.

- Hong Kong uses HKD and Macao uses MOP (HKD is widely accepted in Macao). Your Amex Platinum and no-FTF cards are fine in hotels; mobile pay covers the rest.

- Notify your banks of China / Hong Kong / Macao travel dates.

### Connectivity

Mainland China blocks Google and all Meta apps. See the full **Trip preparations** section overleaf - the short version: install a travel eSIM and two VPN apps **before** departure (you cannot download them once there), and download offline maps for Beijing, Huangshan, Wuzhen, and Hangzhou in Amap.

### Health and comfort

- Bring prescription copies, a small first-aid kit, blister care (Huangshan is stair-heavy), and any altitude/motion medicine your group uses. Tap water in mainland China is not drunk straight - hotels supply boiled or bottled water.

- Travel insurance that covers medical evacuation and trip interruption is worth it for a 15-day, multi-city family trip.

## Trip preparations: connectivity, apps, and customs

The Great Firewall blocks Google, Gmail, YouTube, and all Meta apps on mainland Chinese networks. Set up two independent ways around it **before you fly** - the Play Store and VPN provider websites are blocked once you are there. Hong Kong and Macao have open internet; none of this applies there.

Runner-up: Stellar 30 GB / 20 days at $10.24 (Hong Kong breakout, free VPN included). Cheapest true 3 GB/day plan: Giga.Tel 3 GB/day / 15 days at $14.25, which also covers Hong Kong and Macao. ### VPN as backup - install two before departure

### Translators with camera OCR

- **Google Translate:** download the offline Chinese pack before the flight. Typed translation and the live camera overlay then work fully on-device; voice mode and photo import need Google servers and will not work without a VPN.

- **Pleco** is a fully offline Chinese dictionary; its camera OCR is a paid add-on (about $29.99).

### Power banks — China's 3C rule (matters for your two domestic flights)

- Since June 28, 2025, China's aviation authority (CAAC) bans power banks **without a clear 3C/CCC certification mark** on domestic flights — no mark, a faded/unreadable mark, or a recalled model/batch gets confiscated at security. This applies to your CA1551 (Beijing→Huangshan, Oct 23) and MF8719 (Hangzhou→Macao, Oct 28).
- A US-bought power bank almost certainly lacks the 3C mark — even "Made in China" export models don't carry it. Check yours now: flip it over and look for the 3C/CCC logo plus a legible capacity (mAh/Wh) and model number. If the mark is missing or rubbed off, assume it won't survive PEK or HGH security.
- If yours lacks the mark: buy a 3C-certified power bank **in China** (any convenience store or JD.com) for the domestic legs, or leave yours behind for those flights.
- General limits still apply: under 100 Wh is simplest; 100–160 Wh may need airline approval (max two); over 160 Wh is prohibited. **Carry-on only — never checked.** Keep it switched off and don't charge from it during the flight.

### Customs and cultural notes

- **Chopsticks:** never stick them upright in rice; do not point with them; use the serving chopsticks when a dish provides them.

- **Tipping** is not customary - do not tip; it can confuse rather than please.

- **Bargaining** is expected in markets, not in malls or restaurants.

- **Give and receive** business cards, gifts, and money with two hands.

- **Toilets:** carry tissues; squat toilets are still common outside hotels.

- Public smoking and spitting persist - mildly startling, entirely normal. Avoid sensitive political topics with strangers.

## Arriving in China - arrival card, customs, and HKG transit

The paper arrival card is gone - China went paperless in Nov 2025:

1. **Before landing** (within 24h is ideal): fill the online arrival card at **s.nia.gov.cn/ArrivalCardFillingPC/** or the **NIA 12367 app / NIA mini-program inside WeChat or Alipay**. Need: passport + visa, flight number, and the hotel address (Beijing arrival: Hampton by Hilton Beijing Changping - Changping district). ~3 minutes, 9 languages.
2. **Screenshot the QR code** it gives you (valid 24h) - immigration-hall Wi-Fi is unreliable.
3. At PEK: officer scans the QR; passport open to the visa page. **Fingerprints (both hands, 4+4+thumbs) + photo are collected at the counter** for foreigners 14-70 - no e-channel on a 10-year visa, use the manual counter.
4. **Customs:** green channel if nothing to declare. Red channel if: foreign cash ≥ US$5,000, RMB ≥ 20,000, goods-to-stay ≥ RMB 2,000, alcohol > 1.5L, 400+ cigarettes. The 海关旅客指尖服务 (Customs Pocket Declaration) WeChat/Alipay mini-program handles the declaration online if required. Declare prescription meds and carry the prescription.
5. **HKG transit:** follow "Transfer" signs (Transfer Area E2 for mainland connections) - stay airside, get the CX336 boarding pass at the Cathay transfer desk with passport open to the China visa.

## Beijing - Oct 19 to 23

Your base for three nights, China World Summit Wing (No. 1 Jianguomenwai Avenue, Chaoyang District, Beijing 100004; phone +86 10-6505-2299; check-in 2:00 PM, check-out 12:00 PM), sits on top of the Guomao metro interchange in the CBD, about 15 minutes by car to Tiananmen. Late October is dry and clear but cold: daytime roughly 6-15 C (43-59 F) during your dates, 4-6 C (39-43 F) at night. Dress in layers - this is jacket weather, not shorts weather.

Nanluoguxiang and the Shichahai food stalls - the Oct 21 evening plan.

## Huangshan - Oct 23 to 24

Huangshan's granite peaks above the sea of clouds - the view the whole detour is for.

You land mid-afternoon Fri Oct 23 and have Sat Oct 24 on the mountain. Oct 23 night is at the South Gate (Tangkou); Oct 24 you sleep on the mountain itself, which unlocks the sunrise. Huangshan weather turns fast and the summit is 8-10 C colder than the town - this is the one place on the trip where a warm, waterproof layer is non-negotiable.

### The mountain day (Sat Oct 24)

- **Scenic area hours:** Roughly 6:00-6:30 AM to 5:30 PM in season; cable cars run similar hours, stopping around 5:00-5:40 PM. Confirm the day's times on the official WeChat mini-program the night before.

- **Tickets:** Scenic entry about CNY 190 in season (valid 3 days), scenic shuttle about CNY 19 each way from Tangkou transfer centre. Yungu (Cloud Valley) cable car about CNY 80 one-way; Yuping (Jade Screen) cable car about CNY 90 one-way; West Sea canyon sightseeing train about CNY 100 one-way.

- **Classic route:** Shuttle to Yungu station, cable car up (Back Mountain - gentler start), walk via Beginning-to-Believe Peak and Beihai to Bright Summit (1,860 m, the second-highest peak), then south to the Guest-Greeting Pine. About 5-6 hours with photo stops, mostly paved steps - thousands of them. Overnight near the summit instead of descending.

- **Sunrise:** You are sleeping on the mountain, so set the alarm - the sea-of-clouds sunrise is the whole point of the overnight. Bright Summit and Refreshing Terrace are the classic viewpoints; check with the hotel which is open.

### Practical notes

- Book entry plus cable cars in advance on the official mini-program; holiday queues at Yungu can run over an hour.

- Carry water and snacks up - summit prices are steep and options thin. Trekking poles genuinely help on the descents; buy cheap ones in Tangkou.

- Luggage stays at the South Gate hotel; bring an overnight daypack up the mountain.

## Wuzhen and Hangzhou - Oct 25 to 27

Wuzhen Xizha after dark - stay for the evening, this is when the town earns its reputation.

### Wuzhen - Sun Oct 25 (Crowne Plaza Wuzhen)

- **Xizha (West Scenic Zone):** Open about 9:00 AM-10:00 PM; entry around CNY 150, combined Xizha plus Dongzha ticket around CNY 190. Rowing boat about CNY 60 per person (shared) or CNY 480 per boat.

- **Do:** Arrive mid-afternoon, wander the lanes and dyeing workshop while it is light, take a boat at dusk, then walk the lit canals after dinner. ## Hangzhou - Oct 26 to 27

Leifeng Pagoda over West Lake, Hangzhou - aim for this light on Oct 26.

### West Lake and Lingyin - Mon Oct 26 to Tue Oct 27

- **West Lake:** Free and always open. Walk or cycle the Su Causeway, take a rowing boat from Zhongshan Park wharf, and be on the east shore for sunset behind Leifeng Pagoda.

- **Lingyin Temple and Feilai Peak** (1 Fayun Lane, Lingyin Road, Xihu District): roughly 7:30 AM-5:30/6:00 PM, last entry about 5:00-5:30 PM; Feilai Peak scenic ticket about CNY 45 plus Lingyin Temple about CNY 30 (around CNY 75 total), booked via the West Lake scenic WeChat channels. Go at opening on Oct 27 - it is a major pilgrimage site and crowds build fast. Dress modestly; no photos inside halls.

### Songcheng "Romance of the Song Dynasty" show (Oct 27) - how to book

1. Book on **Trip.com**: search "Hangzhou Songcheng Park Romance Show Ticket" - instant confirmation in English, ~US$42 (¥300) with park entry, free cancellation until 11:00 on the day. Pick the ~7:30 PM show (showtimes confirm ~3 days out on songcn.com/service; typical sessions ~14:00 and 19:30).

Note: Longjing/Meijiawu is a 50-70 min drive from the Xiaoshan airport hotel - go directly there from the tea villages, or via West Lake, rather than doubling back.

## Practical tips, etiquette, and open decisions

### Getting around in mainland China

- **DiDi** is the ride-hailing default; hotels will write destination names in Chinese characters for drivers - screenshot them.

- **Metro** in Beijing and Hangzhou is cheap, signed in English, and paid by Alipay/WeChat transit QR codes.

- **Real-name everything:** Trains, flights and major attractions tie tickets to passports. Keep passports on you (securely) every sightseeing day, not in the hotel safe.

### Etiquette worth knowing

- Temples: modest dress, hats off inside halls, no photography where signed, walk clockwise where locals do.

- Tea and dining: let the eldest pour/be served first; tapping two fingers on the table is a silent thank-you for tea refills in the south (Cantonese custom).

- Queueing and crowd flow at major sites is assertive - stay together, pick a regrouping point at every big attraction.
