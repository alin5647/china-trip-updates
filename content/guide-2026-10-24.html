06:30 Segment 1: South Gate Transfer Center → shuttle bus

🎧 Hear your guide — Transfer Center & shuttle
Walk ~500 m (or a short taxi) from the Holiday Inn Express to the South Gate Transfer Center — H1 at the bottom of the official map. Private cars go no further uphill; every visitor switches to the park shuttle here. Shuttle to Yungu Temple (the Yungu cableway lower station): ¥19, ~20–25 min.

What you'll see: The big visitor hub at the mountain's South Gate in Tangkou — ticket gates, shuttle bays, last shops for raincoats and trekking poles. Scan your passport against your pre-booked ticket and board the Yungu-line bus.

07:10 Segment 2: Yungu Cableway up → Bai'e Ridge

🎧 Hear your guide — Yungu Cableway & Bai'e Ridge
Yungu cable car up: ¥80 one-way, ~8 min, to Bai'e Ridge (White Goose Ridge) station on the back mountain. ✅ The group can split from here.

What you'll see: A wide stone platform right off the cable car, signboards for Shixin Peak and Beihai, and the first gnarled summit pines. On a wet day you may ride up through the cloud layer — the car can break into clear air above a sea of cloud, or arrive in full fog. Both are normal Huangshan.

Segment 3: Shixin Peak (Start-to-Believe Peak)

🎧 Hear your guide — Shixin Peak

What you'll see: A sharp, jagged granite platform with drops on three sides and ancient twisted pines growing straight out of bare rock, wooden railings along the edges. The local saying: "Not seeing Shixin Peak = not seeing Huangshan pines." Less crowded than the Welcoming Pine — take your pine photos here. In fog, the pines appear and vanish in the mist, which photographs beautifully close-up.

Segment 4: Black Tiger Pine

🎧 Hear your guide — Black Tiger Pine

What you'll see: A huge, thick-trunked ancient pine right on the main trail between Shixin Peak and Beihai, with unusually dark, dense foliage and a large information plaque beside it. You walk directly past it — a quick photo stop, no detour.

Segment 5: Beihai Scenic Zone

🎧 Hear your guide — Beihai

What you'll see: A wide open mountain basin of viewpoints, including the Monkey Watching the Sea — a natural rock outcrop shaped like a seated monkey gazing over the valley. With a sea of clouds below it's one of the classic Huangshan compositions (today's odds: 45%). In thick fog you may barely make out the rock — that's the cue to keep moving toward Bright Summit rather than waiting.

Optional, Andrew + Justin ONLY, and only if it's clear: Paiyun Pavilion + West Sea Grand Canyon tram (¥100 one-way). In rain or dense fog skip it entirely — the canyon trails get dangerously slippery and the views vanish. Mom: skip in all weather, stay on the main trail to Bright Summit.

11:30 Segment 6: Bright Summit — Meeting Point #1

🎧 Hear your guide — Bright Summit meet-up
📌 MEET: Bright Summit （光明顶）, 11:30–12:00. Look for the weather-station building and the big stone marker engraved 光明顶 on the open, windy plateau. Fog backup: Baiyun Hotel lobby (marker 5 on the map, a few minutes away) — if you can't find landmarks, go indoors there and share your live location.

12:00–13:00 Lunch: eat your packed snacks at Bright Summit / Baiyun. Hot meals exist in the mountain buildings as an expensive backup. On a clear day the plateau overlooks Lotus Peak and Aoyu Peak; in fog, expect wind and white-out — keep the windproof layer on.

13:00 Segment 7: Aoyu Peak

🎧 Hear your guide — Aoyu Peak

What you'll see: A narrow ridge trail shaped like a giant fish's back. Two ways through: the steep "One-Line-Sky" slot, or the Aoyu Cave tunnel — take the cave, it's the gentler choice for Mom and the safer one on wet stone. Steps here get very slippery when damp; use the handrails.

Segment 8: Hundred-Step Stairway （百步云梯）

🎧 Hear your guide — Hundred-Step Stairway

What you'll see: A very steep, narrow stone staircase carved straight into a vertical cliff face — rock wall on one side, deep drop on the other. It's short but it's the most exposed section of the day. Slow, one step at a time, hand on the rail, especially wet. Let faster hikers pass at the landings.

Segment 9: Welcoming Pine + Yuping Cableway down

🎧 Hear your guide — Welcoming Pine & the descent
📌 HARD FINAL MEET: Yuping Cableway upper station, NO LATER THAN 16:15. The cableway closes ~17:40 — the buffer is for slow, slippery steps. Anyone falling behind: text the group your location immediately.

What you'll see: The Welcoming Pine （迎客松） beside Yuping Building — Huangshan's icon, a single ancient pine with one long branch stretched out like it's greeting guests. Always crowded: for a cleaner photo, shoot from the side platform instead of the packed front spot.
16:15–16:40 Yuping cable car down (¥90) to Ciguang Pavilion (Mercy-Light Pavilion), then shuttle bus (¥19) back to the South Gate Transfer Center. ~17:10–17:40 back at the Holiday Inn Express, Tangkou. Never hike all the way down on foot in the rain — always cable down.

🌧️ Fog / heavy-rain rules

1. Cancel the West Sea Grand Canyon + tram — slippery trails, zero views, save the ¥100.
2. Stick to the wide main paved trail only: Shixin Peak → Beihai → Bright Summit → Aoyu Peak → Welcoming Pine. Skip small side cliff viewpoints.
3. Add 30–45 min to all walking times — wet granite steps slow everyone down.
4. Use building lobbies (Baiyun Hotel, Bright Summit buildings) as meeting points instead of open peaks when visibility collapses.
5. Anyone fatigued or caught in worsening rain: turn toward the Yuping cable station early. Always descend by cable car, never on foot.

📸 Photo tips for a wet summit day

1. In fog and rain the panoramas disappear — shoot close-ups: pine silhouettes, rock textures, mist-wreathed cliffs.
2. Lenses fog constantly in the damp mountain air — wipe with the cloth at every stop.
3. Raincoat beats umbrella everywhere up here; gusts on the open peaks will take an umbrella out of your hands.
4. Welcoming Pine: use the side platform for a clean frame without the crowd.
